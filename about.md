I make music for/with humans and/or machines.

I make music to explore.

I make music because I’m restless and curious about history, technological systems, and how music became what it is.

I decompose to understand what came before.

I compose to imagine new possibilities.

Early encounters with Pauline Oliveros influenced my interest in listening, notation, and the social dynamics of music. I later helped prepare her books *Sounding the Margins* and *Anthology of Text Scores* for publication.

My recent projects engage with the mechanization of musical time, nonlinear interactive scores, imaginary field recordings, and religious ritual. I have been fortunate to collaborate with performers like Khemia Ensemble, Akropolis Reed Quintet, percussionist Abby Fisher, and cellist Ashley Walters. I have presented projects across North America at the Wintergreen Music Festival, Open Ears, nief-norf, the University of Georgia New Music Festival, and ArtX Arizona, and other venues.

Social and historical research informs my creative process, and I have given presentations at the Mid-Atlantic Chapter of the Society for Ethnomusicology Conference, the IEEE Visualization Arts Program, and the International Conference on Live Coding.

I am a builder and maker of sonic things, computer things, electrical things, wooden things, and metal things. Sometimes these things serve musical purposes. Sometimes they don’t. Years ago, I renovated part of an old warehouse to build the Chair Company, a music studio and artist space in Milwaukee’s Harbor District. I currently help students develop their own building skills at UVA’s Maker Open Studio.

I am a Ph.D. student in the Composition & Computer Technologies Program at the University of Virginia. I lived in Wisconsin for many years before moving to Charlottesville and worked as a recording engineer and commercial composer alongside other odd jobs. I have master’s degrees in musicology and composition/technology from the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee and a B.M. in music theory and composition from Lawrence University.
